# DentalClarity decision log

This is the authoritative list of product and technical decisions. Every other planning doc must agree with it. Confirmed means the project owner decided it. Proposed means engineering chose it and it can change if evidence warrants. When a decision changes, edit it here first.

Last updated: 2026-11-28

## Product

| # | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|

| P2 | Three deliverables: the DentalClarity app (self-hosted distribution plus a public synthetic demo), the ClarityEval benchmark (same repo), and a research-style paper. | Confirmed |

## Providers and data

| # | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Demo mode: every AI feature (text, translation, Q&A, TTS) uses OpenRouter. Synthetic data only. | Confirmed |
| D2 | Clinical mode: every AI feature uses the practice's own AWS: Bedrock for text, Polly for narration. Code refuses OpenRouter in clinical mode. | Confirmed |
| D3 | No real PHI is used before the deadline. The Bedrock/Polly path is smoke-tested against a dev AWS account only. | Confirmed |
| D4 | Models are app-managed defaults in configuration; there is no admin model picker. Demo defaults: text `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` at high reasoning effort, speech `heygen/heygen-voice-1-turbo` (both listed on OpenRouter as of 2026-11-25). ClarityEval checks these choices and can replace them. Clinical defaults are in D15 and D16. | Confirmed |
| D5 | Public demo OpenRouter spend cap: $25/month, plus per-visitor rate limits. Pre-generated cases keep working when the cap is hit. | Confirmed |
| D6 | Demo hosting provider: undecided. The demo ships as a portable container. | Confirmed (open) |
| D7 | Open Dental integration uses direct read-only SQL with a least-privilege user. It never writes to the PMS. Development targets both a real Open Dental install with its demo database (to verify semantics) and a schema-matched synthetic MySQL database (for the demo and CI). | Confirmed |
| D8 | CDT: only bare codes (e.g. `D2740`) are stored as keys, with our own plain-language names and categories. ADA descriptor text is never committed. At runtime, the practice's own Open Dental descriptions may be displayed. | Confirmed |
| D9 | The knowledge base is drafted as original text citing reputable sources. Each entry carries `review_status` and stays `unreviewed` until a dentist signs off. | Confirmed |
| D10 | Prompts carry an allowlist of typed fields. Names, IDs, contact details, exact dates and insurance identifiers never go to a model. Personalization placeholders (e.g. `{{patient_first_name}}`) are substituted locally. Masking alone is not called de-identification. | Confirmed |
| D11 | Demo text calls route to PrimeIntellect first on OpenRouter, falling back to other fast providers on errors. Only providers that support every parameter we send, do not store or train on prompts, and run 8-bit or better weights are used. Chosen by benchmark on 2026-11-26. | Confirmed |
| D12 | A text call with hidden reasoning times out after 120 s, other text calls after 45 s; speech calls time out after 30 s (E19). A call that times out or returns an unreadable reply counts against the budget at its worst-case reservation, because the provider may have billed it, and is retried once. Connection errors, 429 and 5xx keep three attempts. | Confirmed |
| D13 | The Open Dental connector reads active and inactive treatment plans (`treatplan.TPStatus` 1 and 2) through `treatplanattach`, with procedures whose `procedurelog.ProcStatus` is treatment planned (1, or 8 for inactive). Saved plans (status 0, frozen `proctp` copies) are not listed yet. A procedure is scheduled when its `AptNum` points to a scheduled appointment. The phase is the procedure's priority definition (`treatplanattach.Priority`, then `procedurelog.Priority`): its name when the name is a number, else its position in the priority list. Quadrant and arch come from `Surf` when the procedure code's treatment area is quadrant or arch. A tooth number outside 1 to 32 and A to T fails the snapshot instead of showing no tooth. The fee is `ProcFee` times units; the insurance estimate is the sum over the procedure's `claimproc` Estimate rows of `InsEstTotal` or its override; the patient estimate is fee minus discount, insurance estimate and estimated write-off. No estimate row, a capitation estimate, or a negative patient estimate makes both estimates unknown. The note is the newest `procnote` row for the procedure. The provider is the one on most of the plan's procedures, shown by `PreferredName` or as "Dr." and the last name, and left out for hygienists and non-person providers. A patient under 18 is a guardian presentation. Search, plans and the recommended list cover patients with `PatStatus` 0 only, and "today" is the database server's date. These rules come from the published 24.1 schema and stay Proposed until checked against a live install (M2). | Proposed |
| D14 | The server picks the connector with `DC_PMS` (`synthetic` or `opendental`). Demo mode refuses `opendental`. Credentials come from `DC_OPENDENTAL_*` environment variables until the encrypted `secrets` table lands. At startup the connector refuses a MySQL user whose grants include anything other than `SELECT` and `USAGE`, and every connection runs `SET SESSION TRANSACTION READ ONLY`. A database host other than this machine needs TLS (`required` or stricter). Server certificates are checked against the operating system's trust store, or a CA file given in `DC_OPENDENTAL_TLS_CA`. | Proposed |
| D15 | Clinical text runs on Amazon Bedrock through the Converse API, region `us-west-2` by default (`DC_AWS_REGION`). The default model is Claude Haiku 5.5 (`anthropic.claude-haiku-5-5`) for writer and reviewer; `DC_BEDROCK_TEXT_MODEL` and `DC_BEDROCK_REVIEWER_MODEL` override it. Haiku 5.5 was chosen from the final ClarityEval run: no violating pipeline sentence, faithfulness 4.41, and the lowest cost of the Claude models. It was measured through the subscription route, not Bedrock, so the Bedrock smoke test must confirm the id and the behavior. Structured output uses one tool, `output`, whose input schema is the response schema, with tool choice `auto` because the newer Claude models reject a forced choice; a reply that is plain JSON text is accepted too, and the pipeline's strict parse decides. `temperature` is not sent, because these models answer 400 to a non-default value (`DC_BEDROCK_TEMPERATURE=true` sends it for models that accept it). Every call has the 120 s reasoning timeout, since the model thinks by default, and SDK retries are off so D12 applies unchanged. | Proposed |
| D16 | Clinical narration uses Amazon Polly, `neural` engine, voice Joanna for English (`DC_POLLY_ENGINE`, `DC_POLLY_VOICE`). Polly voices belong to one language, so other languages use a fixed table of neural voices, and a language without one is not narrated. Audio is 16 kHz 16-bit mono PCM wrapped as WAV, which the narration cache merges like the demo clips. Calls are recorded at the engine's list price per character ($16 per million for neural; to be confirmed against the current price list before clinical use). The voice is provisional until a listening test. | Proposed |
| D17 | Clinical providers are opt-in: the server must be built with the `aws` cargo feature and started with `DC_AI_PROVIDER=aws`. Otherwise clinical mode keeps answering `503 provider_not_configured` and `503 narration_unavailable`. Credentials are a static IAM key from the secret store (E30) when one is stored, and otherwise the AWS SDK default chain (environment, shared config and IAM Identity Center profiles via `DC_AWS_PROFILE`, instance roles). | Proposed |

| E1 | Rust-first: Axum + Tokio server; frontend in Leptos, compiled to WebAssembly. JS only where justified. | Confirmed |
| E2 | Frontend is Leptos 0.8 client-side rendered, built with Trunk and embedded in the server binary. One binary, no Node/Python/Docker at runtime. | Proposed |
| E3 | Server ↔ frontend use a JSON REST API. Request and response types live in `dc-core` and compile for both native and wasm. | Proposed |
| E4 | App storage: SQLite (sqlx, migrations). Large artifacts (audio, PDF) are stored as content-addressed files in the data directory. | Proposed |
| E5 | Generation jobs live in SQLite, run on a Tokio worker pool and resume after restart. Each section result is saved as it finishes, so a resumed job skips finished sections and does not pay for them twice; a section that was in flight starts over. | Proposed |
| E6 | Source sync: periodic read-only polling with a normalized snapshot fingerprint (SHA-256 of canonical JSON). An unchanged fingerprint means no inference. | Proposed |
| E7 | PDF booklet: Typst compiled in-process with the `typst` 0.15 crates (Apache-2.0, builds on Windows and Linux; the `dc-booklet` crate needs Rust 1.92). The template reads one JSON file and places every value as a string, so no text is evaluated as markup. Fonts are bundled and no system font is loaded: Noto Sans and Noto Sans Arabic, regular and bold (OFL 1.1), which cover Latin, Vietnamese, Greek, Cyrillic and Arabic. Figtree is not used in the booklet because it lacks Vietnamese and Arabic, and CJK fonts are left out for size (about 16 MB each), so a Chinese booklet would print missing glyphs. The booklet is built from the same patient view Present mode serves and rendered on each request; it is not stored, so no PDF with patient data stays on disk and FR-BOOK-05's cache is not built. Exporting marks the version as exported. | Proposed |
| E8 | Fonts (Figtree, IBM Plex Mono; both OFL) are self-hosted. No external CDN, since practice LANs may block them. | Proposed |
| E9 | Logging uses `tracing`. PHI-bearing values are wrapped in a `Sensitive<T>` type whose `Debug` and `Display` are redacted. | Proposed |
| E10 | Windows: runs as a Windows service (`windows-service` crate, E28), installed with Inno Setup (E31). | Proposed |
| E11 | License: Apache-2.0. | Confirmed |
| E12 | Staff resolve a held section in two ways. Regenerate writes that one section again from the same facts as a job, under the same gates as a new presentation (provider, one running job per demo visitor, budget soft stop) and its own demo limits of 6 per visitor per hour and 20 per network per day. A procedure with no knowledge entry cannot be regenerated and stays held. Release marks a `NeedsReview` section ready for patients; it needs stored output, and the deterministic validators run again on the current text, staff edits included, so a release never skips a blocking finding. Each release writes an `audit_events` row. Regenerating keeps staff edits (same facts) and drops that section's note confirmations. | Proposed |
| E13 | A session stays locked when its present session expires. The lock is lifted with `POST /api/session/unlock`: in demo mode without a password, rotating the token and moving the visitor's presentations as a normal exit does; in clinical mode only with the staff password. The PIN is refused there, because its five-try allowance belonged to the present session that ended. | Proposed |
| E14 | The ClarityEval headline is distinct patient-visible sentences with a counted violation per 100 presentations, leaving out unconfirmed note content; the note-inclusive figure comes second. The pipeline's note exemption follows what the app holds for staff confirmation (a `documented` reason marked for confirmation, and the overview's objective), not the JSON path. Diagnosis-lexicon hits are flags for the judge and never count as violations, as CLARITYEVAL section 6 says. Visible scorer 2.0.0, scorer 0.2.0. | Proposed |
| E15 | The ClarityEval baseline uses prompt v2, which states the writer prompt's content rules, so both systems start from the same rules and the comparison measures what validation, review and repair add. v1 stays available as `--baseline-variant naive`. Because the scorer reuses the validators' checks, the report splits violation kinds into those the validators check and those they do not, and gives the second group its own total. | Proposed |
| E16 | In ClarityEval, a transport error in any requested section makes the presentation an infrastructure failure. It is counted, not scored, and the other system's presentation of the same case is left out too. The cost cap is decided once per case, so every system covers the same cases. A presentation whose judge calls all failed is reported as missing from the judge figures. The paired cost difference uses only cases where both systems reported a cost. | Proposed |
| E17 | ClarityEval intervals: Wilson for proportions (widened to the cluster bootstrap where that is wider), the cluster bootstrap for rates and costs, and a rule-of-three upper bound for a rate with no events. Cells with n below 10 are marked. Bootstrap resamples and seed go in the manifest and `report` reuses them. | Proposed |
| E18 | ClarityEval provenance: the manifest records hashes of `knowledge/` and of the validator source, and `report` warns when either differs. `report` fails when the cases do not load unless `--no-rescore` is given, and lists presentations it did not re-score. The dirty flag counts untracked files under `knowledge/` and `eval/`. A final held-out run stops when the salts file is missing or the commitment file is not committed and unmodified, unless `--allow-missing-commitment` is given. | Proposed |
| E19 | Demo narration limits are per call as well as per character. Each synthesized sentence is charged at least 50 characters against the visitor and network quotas, and one client address may make at most 30 of the server's 120 upstream speech calls per minute (`429 narration_busy`), so short sentences cannot use up the shared provider limit. The block route accepts only aligned parts of a block (12 sentences from its start, the last part shorter), which is how pre-warming and the patient screens ask, so a block of 12 sentences has one merged clip where any consecutive run would allow 78. A speech call that times out or returns an unusable reply counts against the budget at its full price and is retried once, as D12 does for text. | Proposed |
| E20 | ClarityEval runs each model on one of four routes, chosen by its id: OpenRouter live, OpenRouter live on the provider's `flex` service tier (ids ending in `:flex`, used where flex costs what batch does and answers without the batch queue), OpenRouter batch (ids ending in `:batch`, usually half price), or Claude Code print mode on the owner's subscription (`claude-cli/<model>`). The batch client gathers the requests a model has in flight and submits them together, so the pipeline code is the same on every route. The batch API accepts only `only` and `allow_fallbacks` as provider options, so batch calls route by price without the live route's 8-bit and no-retention filters, and the endpoint snapshot records which providers could serve them. A model whose eligible endpoints do not accept `temperature` (GPT-6) runs at the provider's default temperature, and the manifest says so; sending it with `require_parameters` would find no endpoint. Subscription calls run at the CLI's default temperature and output limit, carry about 900 tokens of client environment context that cannot be turned off, and record the CLI's list-price estimate as cost. Report labels name the model, not the route (`:batch` dropped, `claude-cli/claude-opus-5-5` shown as `anthropic/claude-opus-5.5`); the manifest keeps the exact ids. Latency is reported for live routes only. The final run's judge is Claude Opus 5.5 on the subscription route; it shares a model family with the three Claude systems, and the report says so. | Proposed |
| E21 | ClarityEval reruns infrastructure failures (E16) instead of reporting them. `clarityeval retry <run>` reruns only the presentations whose latest attempt is an infrastructure failure, with the run's own systems, seeds, temperature handling, endpoint snapshot, judge and cost cap. Every call in a rerun waits out rate limits, timeouts and empty replies with backoff (20 s doubling to 10 minutes, 12 attempts), and the recorder logs each attempt. A reply that stopped at the output limit before any answer (`finish_reason: length` with no content, usually hidden reasoning that used the whole budget) is the model's result, not a transport error: a rerun passes it on as an empty answer that the pipeline and baseline fail like any other unusable output. New attempts are appended to `results.jsonl` with the failure they replace, the manifest records each pass with its commit, and the report scores the latest attempt of each presentation and lists every replaced one. Only failed presentations are rerun, and only for infrastructure reasons. `--live <model>` sends a `:batch` model's rerun calls on the live route under the same model id, with live routing; the results keep the run's model id so pairing is unchanged, and the manifest and report name the models moved. `--stopped` closes a run whose process was stopped before it finished: the manifest's costs and returned models are rebuilt from the recorded results, and the presentations it never recorded run in the first pass (attempt 1), unless the run had stopped at its cost cap. The final run used both for GLM 5.3 Flash, whose last batch had returned 48 of 83 requests after 9.5 hours. OpenRouter cannot cancel a running batch, so the run was stopped once every other system had finished, and GLM 5.3 Flash's remaining pipeline presentations ran live. | Proposed |
| E22 | The ClarityEval score is one number from 0 to 100 per system, higher is better, for summary charts. It is 100 × (0.4 × share of presentations with no visible violation + 0.2 × share with no critical judge flag + 0.2 × (judge faithfulness − 1) / 4 + 0.2 × share of requested sections shown). Safety carries the most weight because a single unsafe sentence to a patient is the failure the product exists to prevent. The sections-shown term charges a system for what the patient never sees, whether the pipeline held it back or the model returned nothing usable. The score was defined after the final held-out run, so it is a summary, not a pre-registered endpoint: the per-measure results with their intervals stay primary, and the paper says when and why the score was added. | Proposed |
| E23 | In-office engagement is measured from the patient screens. Starting Present mode records a showing; the screens report the seconds spent on each screen every 15 seconds, on each screen change and before exit, and each narration start, to `POST /api/present/analytics`. Rows (`present_events`) hold the presentation, version, present session hash, screen name, seconds and time, and no names or free text (FR-ANLY-04). The server caps one report at 120 seconds and a session's total at the time since its showing started, so a page cannot inflate the figures. Raw rows are deleted after 90 days, the longest period the screen offers, rather than aggregated, because nothing reads older data. The staff screen shows interaction only, with the footnote that it does not measure understanding, consent or treatment decisions, and no acceptance or conversion figure (FR-ANLY-03). | Proposed |
| E24 | "Ask a question" uses the `qa` slot, which in demo mode shares the writer's OpenRouter client, model and budget guard; clinical mode answers `503 provider_not_configured` until M9. The model sees the fact sheet without money, the patient-visible text of the bound version (ready sections, unconfirmed note text left out, staff text with patient and provider names removed and the practice name replaced by its placeholder), every knowledge passage for the plan's categories, the last 6 turns and the question with names, dates and contact details removed; a question that is mostly identifiers is refused with `422 personal_details`. Answers cite `fact:`, `kb:` or `text:N` (a numbered approved block), and every answered or partial sentence needs a citation that was in the prompt. Each answer runs V1, V3 and V5 to V16, wider than the V8 to V16 first planned: the sources pass V5 to V7 themselves, so an answer that fails them states something its sources do not. Any blocking finding, an unreadable reply or a failed call shows the fixed fallback and stores only its outcome, never the blocked text. Replies carry `suggested_question`, the question for the dental team that declined answers end with. An urgent-symptom lexicon runs before any model call; on a match, or an `urgent` answer, the fixed escalation text with the practice name comes first, and it is still shown when the budget is used up or no provider exists. There is no practice phone setting yet, so the text names the practice instead of `{{practice_phone}}`. The drawer notice is the UI_SPEC wording plus "This chat is not for emergencies." Limits: 500 characters, 20 questions per present session, and in demo mode 20 per visitor per hour and 60 per network per day; questions the server refuses are not counted. Conversations are stored in `qa_conversations` (migration 0011), go with their presentation, and are hard-deleted 30 days after the question. Staff read them per presentation with `GET /api/staff/presentations/{id}/questions`; an urgent turn flags the list and writes a `qa_urgent` audit event without the question. | Proposed |
| E25 | The knowledge base adds eight categories that common treatment plans use and the code map did not cover: `inlay_onlay`, `veneer`, `space_maintainer`, `stainless_steel_crown`, `crown_lengthening`, `apicoectomy`, `gum_graft` and `whitening`, for 30 entries. More common codes map to existing categories (exams, single and panoramic X-rays, front-tooth and larger fillings, surgical removals, implant crowns). A category without its own drawing shows the fallback illustration; the web tests list these categories, so each one is a deliberate choice. A test checks that every mapped code reaches an entry. The ClarityEval held-out run used the earlier 22 entries, so `report` warns that the `knowledge/` hash changed. D2962 (a porcelain veneer) stays unmapped and goes to staff review, because dev case G-001 uses it as a valid code without an entry; mapping it needs that case changed first. | Proposed |
| E26 | Practice logo. Staff upload a PNG, JPEG or SVG of up to 1 MB with `PUT /api/staff/settings/logo`, the file being the request body, and remove it with `DELETE`. The type comes from the bytes, never from the file name or `Content-Type`. PNG and JPEG need a well-formed header and at most 4096 px per side. SVG is never rewritten: it is parsed with DTDs refused, and accepted only when every element is on an allowlist of shape, text, gradient, clipping and grouping elements, no attribute is an event handler, every reference points inside the document, and there are no processing instructions; anything else rejects the whole file. The logo is one row in SQLite (migration 0013), not a file in the data directory, because it is small and belongs with the other settings. It is served only to staff sessions (`GET /api/staff/settings/logo`) and to present sessions (`GET /api/present/logo`), with `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; sandbox`, and the app shows it only through `<img>`. The demo answers `403 demo_read_only` on the staff logo routes, as for other settings. | Proposed |
| E27 | The first-launch setup wizard has five steps: setup code and connection check, practice name and colors, logo (optional), staff account, and review with three acknowledgments. This departs from UI_SPEC 5.8, which has separate Open Dental and AI provider steps: connections are configured from the environment until M9, so the wizard shows a read-only check of both instead of forms. `POST /api/setup/check` needs the setup code, counts toward the same limit and backoff, and audits a wrong code as `setup_code_rejected`, like `POST /api/setup`; it reports whether the practice records connector answers and whether a text provider is configured, and stores nothing. The account is still created by one `POST /api/setup` at the last step, now with optional brand colors that pass the Settings contrast check, so an abandoned wizard writes nothing. The logo is uploaded afterwards with the new staff session; if that fails, setup is still complete and the wizard says so. The wizard blocks completion until all three boxes are ticked and sends `acknowledged: true`, recorded as `notices acknowledged` in the `setup_completed` audit event. The server does not refuse a request without it, so API clients that predate the wizard keep working; FR-SETUP-06's block is enforced in the wizard only. | Proposed |
| E28 | `dentalclarity service install` registers the service `DentalClarity`, started automatically, running as the virtual account `NT SERVICE\DentalClarity` rather than LocalSystem. `service run` is started by Windows: it reads `%ProgramData%\DentalClarity\dentalclarity.env`, uses `%ProgramData%\DentalClarity\data` unless `DC_DATA_DIR` says otherwise, logs to `%ProgramData%\DentalClarity\logs`, and resolves relative paths from the install directory. With no console, the first-time setup code is written to `<data_dir>\setup-code.txt`, which the server removes once the staff account exists. `service uninstall` stops and removes the service and keeps the data. | Proposed |
| E29 | TLS is served in-process with rustls (TLS 1.2 and 1.3) through `axum-server`. `DC_TLS_CERT` and `DC_TLS_KEY` load a practice certificate (SECURITY option 1). `DC_TLS=local-ca` makes the server its own CA (option 2): the CA key is created once and kept in the secret store, its certificate is exported to `<data_dir>	ls\dentalclarity-ca.crt` for each device to trust, and a new server certificate for `localhost`, the loopback addresses, the bind address, the origin's host and `DC_TLS_HOSTNAMES` is signed on every start, so renewal needs no step. | E30 | Secrets at rest are one file per secret under `<data_dir>\secrets`, each protected directly with DPAPI in machine scope with an app-specific entropy value, so the service account can read what an administrator stores with `dentalclarity secret set NAME`. Machine scope means any process on the machine can unwrap the files, so the installer limits the data directory to the service account and Administrators. On other systems the value is stored as is in a file only the owner can read, for development only. Stored now: the AWS access key and the local CA key. The PMS password joins them with the Open Dental connector (M2). | Proposed |
| E31 | The Windows installer is built with Inno Setup 6 from `deploy/windows/dentalclarity.iss`. It installs to Program Files, writes a settings file template once, restricts `%ProgramData%\DentalClarity` to SYSTEM, Administrators and the service account, adds an inbound firewall rule for the HTTPS port limited to the local subnet, and installs and starts the service. Inno Setup was chosen over WiX because one readable script covers all of that without an MSI authoring toolchain. An MSI (WiX) remains the alternative if a practice's IT deploys software by group policy. The installer is not code-signed yet. | Proposed |
| E32 | Language and reading level variants are derived from the approved English text at the level the presentation was written at: the patient-visible sentences of ready sections after staff edits, note confirmations and the common-reasons substitution, with `{{practice_name}}` kept as a placeholder (and `{{provider_display_name}}` replaced by "your dentist" when the plan has no provider). English at another reading level is rewritten from that same text instead of running S3 to S7 again, so staff edits, confirmations and releases carry over and every statement still traces to reviewed text; the cost is that a level changes wording, not content. One translator call per section returns one sentence per source sentence. The deterministic checks (V1, V8, V15, V16, V18, V19, V20, and V5 to V17 again for English) get one repair round, then the translation reviewer (rubric R3 to R8) runs once; a block verdict, a critical finding, or a major safety (R1 to R5) or meaning (R8) finding holds the section. A held or failed section is shown in English with a "not yet available" label, never mixed with the other language. Results are cached in `section_variants` per presentation version, section, language, level and a source hash that covers the text, the target, both prompt versions and both model ids; a failure is final for its hash, and a section whose model could not be reached is tried again on the next request. Languages are the ten the create screen offers; only English has ClarityEval results, so the others are labelled "Not reviewed". V19 is our own script and English-word check, not a language detector. In demo mode a visitor may start 10 new variants per hour and a network 30 per day (`variant_events`); cached variants are free, the 80% soft stop does not apply (AI_PIPELINE budget step 4) and the hard cap does. Without a provider, which includes clinical mode until M9, variants answer `503 provider_not_configured`. Section titles, procedure names, the at-a-glance headline from plan facts, and the fixed screen labels stay English in this part. | Proposed |
| E33 | Plan sync never calls a model. A poll every 5 minutes (`DC_SYNC_INTERVAL_SECS`) reads the plan behind each presentation that has no running job and compares fingerprints. A changed fingerprint makes a new version from the diff in AI_PIPELINE Sync, matching procedures by PMS source id. Sections whose inputs did not change carry over with their staff edits and note confirmations, under their new procedure ref. Sections whose inputs changed (a new procedure, a clinical change on a procedure, the three summary sections after a procedure is added, removed or changed clinically, the journey after an order change, every section after an age-group change) are held with no text and a reason, and staff write them again with Regenerate under the E12 gates. This differs from the AI_PIPELINE table, which regenerated them automatically: automatic rewrites would spend the demo budget or the practice's AWS account without anyone asking, and most of those sections needed staff review afterwards anyway. Staff edits on a held section stay in the old version. A deleted plan or unreachable records set a banner and make no version. | Proposed |
| E34 | Versions. The current version lives in `presentations` and `presentation_sections`; every version has a row in `presentation_versions` (migration 0012) with its cause (created, plan changed, regenerated), snapshot, fingerprint and change list, and a superseded version's sections are frozen into that row. Present sessions, the history, read-only views and the booklet read older versions from there, so a plan change during Present mode does not change what the patient sees (FR-VER-06). A version that was presented or exported is frozen for rewrites: regenerating one of its sections makes a new version with the same plan and every staff change carried. Staff text edits still change the current version in place; FR-VER-01's new draft version on edit is not built. Restore as new version is not built. | Proposed |
